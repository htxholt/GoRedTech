Business Automation Sprint

Replace a repetitive workflow with a dependable automation.

A focused engagement for one stable, rules-based process that consumes time, creates avoidable errors, or keeps information trapped between tools.

Discuss an automation
Best fit

When the steps are known but the work is still manual.

A strong fit has repeatable inputs, clear decisions, available system access, and an owner who can validate the desired result.

Common workflows

  • Moving or reconciling data between applications
  • Recurring reports, exports, file processing, or status checks
  • Deployment and maintenance routines
  • Notifications, approvals, and cross-application handoffs
  • API, script, RPA, or lightweight workflow integration

Define

Map inputs, decisions, outputs, exceptions, dependencies, ownership, and acceptance criteria.

Build

Implement the agreed automation with appropriate validation, logging, status checks, and error handling.

Verify

Test representative cases, document operation and recovery, and walk the owner through the result.

Process

A controlled sprint, not a black box.

  1. Fit review: confirm the workflow is stable enough to automate.
  2. Scope: agree on one workflow, assumptions, access, and acceptance criteria.
  3. Implementation: build against representative data and available environments.
  4. Validation: verify expected and recoverable failure paths.
  5. Handoff: provide operating notes and next-step recommendations.

Scope boundaries and assumptions

The typical range assumes one defined workflow, timely access, usable test inputs, and no unplanned vendor or platform changes. New paid software, extensive data cleanup, unstable source processes, multiple unrelated workflows, and third-party delays may require revised scope or timing. Automation reduces repeat work but does not eliminate the need for ownership and monitoring.
