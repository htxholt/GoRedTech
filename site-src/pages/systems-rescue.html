Best fit

When symptoms cross systems and the cause is still unclear.

A strong fit includes authorized access, available logs or evidence, a knowledgeable contact, and room to reproduce or safely inspect the failure.

Common systems problems

  • Cross-service startup, DNS, identity, certificate, or dependency failures
  • Broken API, application, database, or vendor integrations
  • Deployment and maintenance failures without reliable recovery controls
  • Linux, Windows, Docker, network, or self-hosted service issues
  • Recurring production incidents that need evidence-led diagnosis

Diagnose

Collect symptoms, sequence events, inspect logs and dependencies, and test the strongest hypotheses.

Stabilize

Apply agreed, reversible changes or controls when the cause and safe path are sufficiently clear.

Validate

Use status or health checks to verify behavior and document recovery, residual risk, and next steps.

Process

Reduce risk while narrowing the unknowns.

  1. Intake: establish impact, timeline, known changes, access, and safety constraints.
  2. Triage: prioritize evidence and identify the likely failure boundary.
  3. Diagnosis: test hypotheses across relevant services and dependencies.
  4. Remediation: make agreed changes when supported by evidence.
  5. Validation: verify service health and document findings or escalation needs.

Scope boundaries and assumptions

The typical range assumes authorized and timely access, available evidence, and a bounded technical problem. Vendor response time, unavailable credentials, damaged data, unsupported platforms, security incidents, hardware replacement, after-hours windows, or broad environment redesign may require separate scope. Diagnosis can narrow or identify a cause, but a complete repair cannot be guaranteed before the system and dependencies are inspected.
