Discuss a website
Best fit

When the business has something valuable to offer but the website makes it hard to understand or act.

A strong fit has a clear decision-maker, available brand/content inputs, a focused page set, and access to the current domain or hosting when launch support is in scope.

Common website problems

  • The offer is buried under generic service language
  • Mobile visitors cannot find or complete the next step
  • Lead paths, calls to action, or contact details are unclear
  • The current site is difficult to update, slow, or inaccessible
  • Multiple tools or forms need a coherent public experience

Message hierarchy, implementation, and launch readiness.

Structure

Clarify priority audiences, offers, page architecture, calls to action, and required content.

Build

Implement a responsive, accessible, performance-conscious site using maintainable assets and code.

Verify

Test key paths and forms, validate links and assets, document the build, and prepare the agreed launch handoff.

Process

Make the visitor journey explicit.

  1. Fit review: identify the business goal and required page set.
  2. Content readiness: confirm approved copy, brand assets, contact details, and access.
  3. Build: create the agreed pages and lead paths.
  4. Review: consolidate stakeholder feedback against the approved scope.
  5. Handoff: verify delivery and document ownership.

Scope boundaries and assumptions

The typical range assumes a focused brochure or lead-generation site, supplied or jointly refined content, one decision-maker, and timely domain/hosting access. E-commerce, custom applications, extensive copywriting, complex migrations, paid services, photography, and large content libraries require separate scope. Search visibility and lead volume depend on factors beyond the site build and are not guaranteed.
